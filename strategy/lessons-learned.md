# Lessons learned and recurring review requirements

Sources: the discussions indexed in [the source register](../SOURCES_AND_LIMITATIONS.md). These are process lessons from the project, not claims that every earlier choice was objectively wrong.

## 1. Keep one dated plan and explain revisions

The user objected to radical changes after two disappointing weeks. Preserve the agreed baseline, show exactly what new evidence changed, and explain the expected benefit of each replacement. Do not silently swap an entire plan late in the week.

## 2. Do not confuse proposed, accepted and submitted

A chat question such as 'confirm?' or 'lock it?' is not proof of submission. Nor is an assistant saying a transfer is completed. Maintain separate decision status and transaction evidence, especially for chips and wildcard rosters.

## 3. Verify starts instead of guessing from a flag

Isak's availability and consistency repeatedly mattered. An injury flag or percentage is not a confirmed absence. The GW7 record includes a recommendation to bench Isak and a later user challenge about his actual start. The submitted fantasy XI and exact lost points are not established; do not publish an invented loss calculation.

## 4. Separate performance from one-week noise

The user challenged keeping Palmer after -1 and removing consistently performing Isak. A bad score alone does not settle the next selection; neither does one big return. For new advice, assess role, minutes, fixture and underlying opportunity rather than just the last points total.

## 5. Audit wildcard affordability using selling values

The user estimated an approximately £0.5m price impact from delay. A later £1.0m spare remark and a £101.4m draft refer to different stages. Record the exact state rather than combining them. Do not declare a complete roster affordable without the real bank and selling prices.

## 6. Evaluate chip value against the correct counterfactual

The user reported Bench Boost in a disappointing GW8. Some bench points might have counted through ordinary autosubs anyway; the discussion does not establish the exact difference. Evaluate all 15 players' minutes and calculate the actual incremental value before declaring success or failure.

## 7. Recheck keeper upgrades against outfield opportunity cost

Alisson, Verbruggen, Trafford and Tzolakis were considered against keeping Kinský/Matthews. The later assistant recommendation kept the cheaper pair. Retain the rationale as a historical recommendation, not a permanent ban on spending on a goalkeeper.

## 8. Respect football preferences explicitly

Liverpool representation, the preferred formation and the Maguire exclusion should be visible in every new plan. Historical 3-4-3 recommendations do not erase those constraints. Flag and explain any exception instead of pretending all constraints are already satisfied.

## 9. Date every source and distinguish facts from projections

Prior expected-points figures were not independently audited. Historical club names, fixtures and injury news may be stale or incorrect. Future research needs fresh sources; preserving an old note is not endorsing it as current advice.

## 10. Preserve outcomes and rival context without fabrication

The archive records an assistant-reported GW6 score of 77 and first place in Venditos, but has no complete official score history or rival table. Add actual post-week results when available and do not invent missing early gameweeks or top-three rival totals.
